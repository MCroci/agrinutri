## 1. Comparable tools and their key papers

### Takeaway
The closest peers fall into four families: (a) regulatory/advisory N-balance calculators (Fert_Office, the national systems reviewed by Jordan-Meille et al. 2023, MANNER-NPK, NDICEA, NUTMON, ValorE); (b) dynamic model-based recommenders (Adapt-N, Yield Prophet/APSIM); (c) sensor algorithms (GreenSeeker/NFOA); (d) open GIS/VRT software (QGIS plugins, Python ports). None of the verified tools combines a legally binding regional mass balance with satellite NNI and VR export in an open R package. That combination is NFert's niche. No peer-reviewed paper on Fert_Office itself was found.

### Inferences
- Pick the comparators by family. NFert's core competitor is the national/regional calculator (Jordan-Meille 2023 shows that Italy's is among the most complex, with 10 variables). Its added value is open, testable code plus a spatial layer. The DOIs above support a feature table with columns: method family, legal status, spatial, RS input, VR export, open source, validation.
- Software-description papers that became highly cited (APSIM, NUTMON-scale) became infrastructure that others reuse. Tool papers with strong validation (Adapt-N, MANNER-NPK) get moderate citations but drive adoption.

## 2. How the most-cited papers framed their contribution and what validation they used

### Takeaway
Two framings dominate. (i) "Framework/software" papers (APSIM, NUTMON) cite their scope and user base and do not run new field validation. (ii) "Tool + evidence" papers (Adapt-N, MANNER-NPK, GreenSeeker) validate against grower practice or measured values. The commonest evidence is a paired comparison with current farmer rates or the official recommendation, reported as N saved, yield difference, profit and simulated losses. Multi-site, multi-year on-farm strips are the gold standard. Colaço & Bramley (2018) criticise comparison against farmer practice alone.

### Cited Findings
- Adapt-N framing: "Current N rate recommendation tools are highly generalized over space and time". It positions the tool as dynamic against static recommendations. Validation: 113 replicated strip trials, tool vs grower rate, with yield, profit and simulated N loss as endpoints — [Crossref abstract](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.2134/agronj2015.0606)
- MANNER-NPK framing: an "enhanced software tool". Validation: predicted vs measured fertiliser-N replacement values from field experiments — [Semantic Scholar](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1111/sum.12078)
- Yield Prophet framing: a participatory, low-intensity internet service succeeding FARMSCAPE. The evidence is user engagement and monitoring value rather than a yield RCT — [Semantic Scholar TLDR](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1071/CP09020)
- Jordan-Meille 2023 validated nothing in the field. Its evidence is a common-scenario cross-comparison: the same farm cases are run through each national method — [Crossref abstract](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1111/ejss.13436)
- Basso et al. 2016, "Environmental and economic benefits of variable rate nitrogen fertilization in a nitrate vulnerable zone", Sci. Total Environ., DOI 10.1016/j.scitotenv.2015.12.104 (cites 177 [189]). It is Italian, set in a nitrate-vulnerable zone and widely cited. The evidence is crop-model simulation (model named SALUS from recall, not confirmed in-session) of VR vs uniform rates per zone, reported as profit and nitrate leaching — [Semantic Scholar TLDR](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1016/j.scitotenv.2015.12.104)
- Vizzari et al. 2019, "Sentinel 2-Based Nitrogen VRT Fertilization in Wheat: Comparison between Traditional and Simple Precision Practices", Agronomy, DOI 10.3390/agronomy9060278 (cites 72). This is the most direct analogue of NFert (Central Italy, 14 ha, 168 randomised units). The flat rate of 120 kg N/ha came from "a common N balance". It was compared with two NDVI linear VR models, one capped at the standard rate and one with its mean equal to the standard rate (cf. NFert's mean-preserving strips). Endpoints were VI, yield maps and protein — [Semantic Scholar](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.3390/agronomy9060278)
- Santaga et al. 2021, "Simplified and Advanced Sentinel-2-Based Precision Nitrogen Management of Wheat", Agronomy, DOI 10.3390/agronomy11061156 (cites 26). Five treatments (flat N-balance, NDVI-direct, NDVI-inverse, AgroSat, Agricolus) in 4 fields over 2 seasons, randomised blocks. There were "no substantial differences in crop yield", and weather dominated. Differences appeared in NUE — [Semantic Scholar](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.3390/agronomy11061156)
- Argento et al. 2020, Precis. Agric., DOI 10.1007/s11119-020-09733-3 (cites 156 Crossref [16 S2], a source discrepancy). Plot experiment comparing uniform and VR N with N-rich and zero-N reference plots, over 2 seasons, using NUE indicators — [Semantic Scholar](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1007/s11119-020-09733-3)
- Lawes & Bramley 2012, "A simple method for the analysis of on-farm strip trials", Agron. J., DOI 10.2134/agronj2011.0155 (cites 35); Bullock et al. 2019, "The Data-Intensive Farm Management Project...", Agron. J., DOI 10.2134/agronj2019.03.0165 (cites 97). Methodological references for on-farm precision experimentation — [Crossref](https://doi.org/10.2134/agronj2019.03.0165)
- Rose et al. 2016, "Decision support tools for agriculture: Towards effective design and delivery", Agric. Syst., DOI 10.1016/j.agsy.2016.09.009 (cites 480 [505]). It lists 15 uptake factors, including usability, cost-effectiveness, performance, relevance and "compatibility with compliance demands", and reports that uptake is low — [Semantic Scholar](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1016/j.agsy.2016.09.009)
- Hochman & Carberry 2011, "Emerging consensus on desirable characteristics of tools to support farmers' management of climate risk", Agric. Syst., DOI 10.1016/j.agsy.2011.03.001 (cites 71) — [Crossref](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agsy.2011.03.001)

### Inferences
- The Rose et al. factor "compatibility with compliance demands" is NFert's strongest framing hook: a DSS that is the compliance calculation (DPI/MAS) removes a known adoption barrier. Cite it in the Introduction.
- Vizzari 2019 and Santaga 2021 show Italian reviewers accept "N-balance flat rate vs VR redistribution" as a design. They also show that yield differences are usually non-significant, so NUE and N saved are the honest endpoints. NFert cannot claim yield benefits without trials. It can claim compliance, reproducibility and mass-balance preservation.
- With one farm and no yield data, NFert fits framing (i), a software/method paper (SoftwareX/EMS style), plus a verification layer (equivalence with Fert_Office) rather than a validation layer. State this explicitly as a limitation and a roadmap.

### Gaps
- The full texts of Basso 2016 and Colaço & Bramley 2018 were not read; their summaries rest on abstract/TLDR and citing papers.

## 3. What reviews of Sentinel-2 NNI / canopy N retrieval and VR-N say is missing

### Takeaway
The reviews converge on these gaps: retrieval-to-decision coupling (estimates of N status rarely become agronomically justified rates), univariate sensor-only decisions that ignore soil and weather, weak economic evidence, comparison only against farmer practice, and limited operational, transferable workflows. A tool that couples S2 retrieval, NNI, a legally grounded balance and machine-readable prescriptions addresses the "operationalisation" gap directly, though not the evidence gap.

### Cited Findings
- Colaço & Bramley 2018, "Do crop sensors promote improved nitrogen management in grain crops?", Field Crops Res., DOI 10.1016/j.fcr.2018.01.007 (cites 170). Most studies report 5-45% N savings with little effect on yield, but there is a lack of consistent evidence of economic benefit. About 25% of studies reported economic losses. Most strategies treat the N decision as univariate (a single sensor type). Testing only against current farm practice makes the benefit depend on how good that practice is. Plot trials poorly reflect field-scale variability — [Scinapse](https://scinapse.io/papers/2793934260); [citing paper, Precis. Agric.](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s11119-023-10102-z)
- Berger et al. 2020, "Crop nitrogen monitoring: Recent progress and principal developments in the context of imaging spectroscopy missions", Remote Sens. Environ., DOI 10.1016/j.rse.2020.111758 (cites 415). It reviewed over 400 studies (125 retained) and grouped retrieval methods into parametric, linear nonparametric, machine-learning (incl. GPR) and RTM/hybrid. The framing is that continuous satellite N monitoring "would enable site-specific N management" — [Semantic Scholar](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1016/j.rse.2020.111758)
- Verrelst et al. 2012, "Machine learning regression algorithms for biophysical parameter retrieval: Opportunities for Sentinel-2 and -3", Remote Sens. Environ., DOI 10.1016/j.rse.2011.11.002 (cites 524); Verrelst et al. 2015, "Optical remote sensing and the retrieval of terrestrial vegetation bio-geophysical properties - A review", ISPRS J., DOI 10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2015.05.005 (cites 780). - Lemaire et al. 2008, "Diagnosis tool for plant and crop N status in vegetative stage", Eur. J. Agron., DOI 10.1016/j.eja.2008.01.005 (cites 721). The NNI foundation — [Crossref](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eja.2008.01.005)
- Delloye et al. 2018, Remote Sens. Environ., DOI 10.1016/j.rse.2018.06.037 (cites 181). Canopy N content vs S2-retrieved canopy chlorophyll: R² = 0.90, errors of 4-21 kg/ha depending on stage. The relation is stage-dependent and works with 10 m bands only, which opens the way for precision agriculture — [Semantic Scholar](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1016/j.rse.2018.06.037)

The authors conclude that S2 NNI could support fertilisation plans without ground measurements — [search result](https://research.unipg.it/handle/11391/1525073)
- Corti et al. 2018, "Does remote and proximal optical sensing successfully estimate maize variables? A review", Eur. J. Agron., DOI 10.1016/j.eja.2018.06.008 (cites 68) — [Crossref](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eja.2018.06.008). - Cilia et al. 2014, "Nitrogen status assessment for variable rate fertilization in maize through hyperspectral imagery", Remote Sens., DOI 10.3390/rs6076549 (cites 169). ### Inferences
- Gaps a tool paper can credibly claim to address: (1) multivariate decisions, since NFert combines soil (balance terms, SoilGrids), crop (NNI) and regulation rather than NDVI alone; (2) an end-to-end operational chain to ISOXML/shapefile, which no open N-balance tool found here provides; (3) explicit regulatory constraints (MAS caps), which the VR literature ignores but Jordan-Meille 2023 shows are legally binding in several countries.
- Gaps NFert does not address and should concede: economic and yield evidence (Colaço & Bramley), multi-year EONR instability (Mamo 2003), and S2 NNI uncertainty from soil background (Crema 2020).

### Gaps
- Reviews specifically on S2-based NNI for maize in Europe were not found. The literature is mostly wheat or crop-agnostic.

## 4. Cheap evidence the NFert authors could add (modelled on comparable papers)

### Takeaway
Without new trials, the evidence that comparable papers used and that NFert can reproduce cheaply is: (a) verification against the official tool; (b) comparison with the farmer's actual N rates; (c) cross-scenario comparison across methods and strategies, as in Jordan-Meille 2023 and Vizzari/Santaga; (d) retrospective checks against yield maps or NNI maps from past seasons; (e) sensitivity and uncertainty of the balance to soil inputs (SoilGrids vs lab data).

### Cited Findings
- Tool vs grower-selected rate is the headline design of the best-known DSS validation (Adapt-N, -34% N, profit and simulated losses) — [Crossref abstract](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.2134/agronj2015.0606). Memic et al. reported simulated optimum vs actually applied uniform rates (-42%/-39%) using only existing field records — [Hohenheim abstract](https://www.uni-hohenheim.de/en/organization/publication/gis-based-spatial-nitrogen-management-model-for-maize)
- Common-scenario comparison across calculation methods, with no field data, was enough for an EJSS paper — [Crossref abstract](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1111/ejss.13436)
- Comparing VR strategies with a capped maximum vs a preserved mean (Vizzari 2019) and direct vs compensative (Santaga 2021) is directly reproducible in NFert as a scenario table: total N, distribution and MAS exceedances per strip — [Vizzari 2019](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.3390/agronomy9060278); [Santaga 2021](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.3390/agronomy11061156)
- Predicted vs measured comparison of one component (MANNER-NPK manure N replacement) is a partial-validation route: e.g., validate only the S2 GPR LAI/CNC module against any available ground or published dataset — [Semantic Scholar](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1111/sum.12078)
- Simulation-based environmental and economic evaluation (Basso 2016, crop model) is an accepted substitute for trials in an Italian NVZ context — [Semantic Scholar TLDR](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1016/j.scitotenv.2015.12.104)
- On-farm strip methodology (Lawes & Bramley 2012; Bullock 2019, DIFM) supports a "planned validation" section, and NFert's strip prescriptions could be designed as such trials — [Crossref](https://doi.org/10.2134/agronj2011.0155); [Crossref](https://doi.org/10.2134/agronj2019.03.0165)

### Inferences
Ranked by cost/benefit for the NFert manuscript:
1. **Verification table, NFert vs Fert_Office v1.26** (the Excel harness already exists): N, P and K doses for N crop × soil cases, with max abs diff. This is near-zero cost, and since Fert_Office appears unpublished it is the only verification of the regional calculation in the literature.
2. **Farmer's actual N vs NFert balance dose vs MAS** for each field of the 32 ha farm (from farm records or as-applied logs), reporting surplus/deficit and compliance. This mirrors Adapt-N/Memic, but must be framed as "comparison", not "validation" (Colaço & Bramley's critique).
3. **Strategy comparison**: uniform balance dose vs NNI-proportional vs NNI-compensative vs capped-max, reporting field mean preserved, CV, % area at MAS cap and N moved between zones (as in Vizzari/Santaga).
4. **Soil-input sensitivity**: SoilGrids vs farm lab analyses (or ± uncertainty bands) propagated to the balance dose. This links to the SAT soil-uncertainty storyline.
5. **Retrospective plausibility**: correlate past-season yield maps (if any) with NNI and prescription zones. This is a weak but cheap check; state it as indicative.
6. **Multi-region or multi-method scenario**: run a Jordan-Meille-style common farm case through NFert to show where the Emilia-Romagna balance sits among the EU methods (moderate cost, high citation appeal).
