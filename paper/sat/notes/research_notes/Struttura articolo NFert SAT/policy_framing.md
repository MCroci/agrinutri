## 1. EU policy context, 2023-2026

### Takeaway
The Commission published the first full evaluation of the Nitrates Directive (ND) on 15 July 2026. It keeps the Directive unchanged: it is still effective and relevant, and the 170 kg N/ha manure cap stays. What the evaluation asks for is "smarter implementation", less administrative burden, and "improving nutrient management at farm level, adapted to local circumstances". The Integrated Nutrient Management Action Plan (INMAP) had still not been delivered as of October 2026. The CAP 2023-27 obliges Member States to offer a digital Farm Sustainability Tool for Nutrients (FaST) "at the latest as from 2024", through the Farm Advisory Services rather than conditionality. Together these give a tool paper a strong hook: auditable, locally adapted, digital farm-level nutrient management.

### Inferences
- The 2026 evaluation shifts the policy question from whether to regulate to how to implement more smartly and locally with less burden. A transparent, open, locally parameterised (regional DPI) calculator fits this framing directly.
- FaST is a legal obligation via Art. 15(4)(g), but its implementation is fragmented: national or regional tools, many not openly documented. NFert can be positioned as an open, reproducible, testable engine that a regional FaST-type service could embed. Claim compatibility only with what Art. 15(4)(g) actually lists; read the minimum-content list first.
- INMAP stalled and the Fertiliser Action Plan has an input-security and RENURE angle, so the Farm to Fork 50% target lacks a delivery instrument. Field-level balance tools are one of the few practical levers. Phrase this cautiously.

## 2. Italian / Emilia-Romagna context

### Takeaway
Emilia-Romagna regulates N at two layers. The first is mandatory: the regional ND action programme and manure-use rules, now Regional Regulation 2/2024 (19 Mar 2024), which makes the Massimo Apporto Standard (MAS) the cap on crop N needs and links it to PUA plans. The second is voluntary but subsidised: the Integrated Production rules (DPI 2026, approved by DGR 3130 of 16 Feb 2026), which require a crop N balance or standard-dose sheets, computed with the region's own FertDPI/Fert_Office tool. The Po plain is a nitrate hotspot with long aquifer legacy times.

Among the main obligations of 2/2024 is applying the MAS when defining crop N needs, and MAS is linked to drafting PUAs. [secondary: ARSIAL conference slides, Feb 2026] — [ARSIAL, Sarno](https://www.arsial.it/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/SARNO.pdf); [ARSIAL, Biondini-Ambrosi](https://www.arsial.it/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/BIONDINI-AMBROSI.pdf). ### Inferences
- The paper should keep two compliance thresholds distinct. The MAS (RR 2/2024) is a legal cap tied to the ND/SMR 2. The DPI balance is a voluntary-scheme requirement tied to CAP agri-environment payments (SRA01 in the Italian CAP Strategic Plan [unverified]). The finding that "the balance exceeds the MAS on 4 of 8 fields" is a conflict between two regulatory layers. Stated carefully, it is a policy-relevant result: a weather-adjusted DPI requirement can exceed the legal ceiling, and in that case the MAS binds.
- Fert_Office/FertDPI is closed and spreadsheet-based. NFert's contribution is an open, version-controlled, unit-tested reimplementation validated against the official tool. That is what auditability means here.

- No Emilia-Romagna-specific peer-reviewed study comparing DPI balance doses with measured crop N needs or EONR was found.

## 3. Uncertainty and weather-dependence in regulatory N balances

### Takeaway
There is a modest literature on uncertainty in farm N budgets. Farm-gate budgets are less uncertain than soil-surface or soil-system budgets, and Monte Carlo is the standard method. Some authors explicitly propose using balance uncertainty to estimate the risk of exceeding permitted surpluses. No regulation found treats uncertainty in a nutrient plan or compliance total explicitly: limits are deterministic. Weather enters the rules only through fixed lookup adjustments (winter-rainfall leaching classes in Italian DPIs; spring Nmin in Germany [unverified]), while the agronomic literature shows the optimum N rate varies strongly from year to year with weather.

### Cited Findings
- The relative uncertainty of nutrient surpluses increases in the order farm-gate budget < soil-surface budget < soil-system budget. Farm-gate budgets are therefore preferred as policy instruments. [secondary: abstract] — Oenema, Kros & de Vries (2003), "Approaches and uncertainties in nutrient budgets: implications for nutrient management and environmental policies", Eur. J. Agron. 20:3-16 (bibliographic details [unverified]) — [academia.edu copy](https://www.academia.edu/26074631/Approaches_and_uncertainties_in_nutrient_budgets_implications_for_nutrient_management_and_environmental_policies)
- Dutch dairy farms: Monte Carlo quantified how input-data uncertainty affects annual farm N surplus and soil-surface N surplus. The authors note the uncertainty of an annual balance "could be used to determine the risk of ... exceeding environmental targets (e.g. permitted N surplus)". [secondary: abstract snippet] — ["Stochastic uncertainty and sensitivities of nitrogen flows on dairy farms in The Netherlands", Agricultural Systems, 2015](https://sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0308521X15000608). Authors not captured. [unverified]
- Manure-based cropping systems: uncertainty in N application "not only impacts farmer practices but also regulatory effectiveness". [secondary] — ["Quantifying the uncertainty in nitrogen application and groundwater nitrate leaching in manure based cropping systems", Agricultural Systems](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0308521X19315525). Authors and year not captured. [unverified]
- Portugal (Tagus basin): the main uncertainty sources included mineral fertilisation, nitrate in irrigation water, and the share of farmers complying with ND measures. [secondary: search snippet; exact paper not identified] [unverified]
- 2026 Nature Food: a 500-run Monte Carlo estimated uncertainty in recoverable manure N and crop N demand across the USA. This is a current example of uncertainty-aware nutrient accounting at policy scale. [secondary] — [Nature Food, s43016-026-01312-5](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43016-026-01312-5)
- Static (yield-goal or empirical) recommendation systems "do not account for year-to-year variability" and give one rate suited at best to an average season. [secondary: Agronomy J. 2018 review of N-rate recommendation strengths and limitations, US-focused] — [Agron. J. 2018 review (Chesapeake Bay copy)](https://dev.chesapeakebay.net/files/documents/2018-agronomy_j-strengths_and_limitations_of_n_rate_recs-review.pdf). Likely Morris et al. 2018. [unverified]
- US maize: in one study, most EONR variability was explained by V5-V12 rainfall (R² = 0.84). [secondary] — [ASA/CSSA/SSSA print PDF](https://www.sciencesocieties.org/print/pdf/node/3011). Reference details not captured. [unverified]
- Mediterranean wheat, 56-year simulation: with low root-zone water at sidedressing, lower N rates raised profit and reduced leaching. [secondary] — [Precision Agriculture, s11119-022-09957-5](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s11119-022-09957-5.pdf)
- Ireland (Teagasc 2025): an environmental and economic assessment of cutting maximum chemical N allowances for arable crops. This is a direct analogue of the MAS/allowance debate. [secondary: title] — [Teagasc 2025 report](https://teagasc.ie/wp-content/uploads/uploads/media/website/publications/2025/An-environmental-and-economic-assessment-on-the-impact-of-possible-reductions-in-the-maximum-chemical-nitrogen-allowances-for-main-arable-crops.pdf)

### Inferences
- NFert adds two things on top of this literature. (i) Weather-dependence: rerunning the regulatory balance with measured 2024-25 rainfall raised it by 26% and pushed 4 of 8 fields over the MAS. Deterministic rules with a single annual rainfall class can thus flip compliance status. (ii) Spatial correlation: farm-total uncertainty depends on the soil-error correlation length. No study found addresses how spatial autocorrelation of input errors scales to the uncertainty of farm-level compliance totals. This looks like a genuine gap, though not exhaustively searched.
- A sound way to frame it: deterministic limits plus uncertain inputs give a probability of non-compliance, not a yes/no answer. This follows the Dutch dairy authors' suggestion and moves it from farm-gate surplus to the field-level regulatory balance.

### Gaps
- No regulation was found that tolerates or adjusts for uncertainty in nutrient plans, for example confidence bands or "precautionary margin" clauses. The German DüV (§3-4: Düngebedarfsermittlung with spring Nmin) and Danish N quotas were not retrieved. Check them before claiming "no regulation addresses uncertainty".
- No European head-to-head study comparing regulatory recommendation systems with measured EONR was found. Candidates: Italian DPI vs field trials (Univ. Padova / Bologna), Danish N-norm validation.

## 4. Open and digital compliance tools and their scientific documentation

### Takeaway
Most national compliance calculators are documented in grey literature (government or institute reports), not peer-reviewed, versioned, open-source software papers. The Dutch KringloopWijzer is the best-documented case, with WUR reports describing its calculation rules. RB209 is a guidance manual, not open code. FaST was meant to be open but is implemented unevenly.

### Cited Findings
- Netherlands, KringloopWijzer (ANCA): its modules are BEX (farm-specific excretion), BEA (ammonia), BEN (N flows), BEP (P flows, not N) and BEC (C). Calculation rules are documented in WUR reports: 2014 rules, 2020 update, and WPR-1546 (2025) updating excretion parameters (doi:10.18174/706975). [verified via WUR records] — [WUR edepot 706975](https://edepot.wur.nl/706975); [ANCA 2020 calculation rules](https://edepot.wur.nl/563400); [Rekenregels KringloopWijzer, AGRIS](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/64746b0b79cbb2c2c1af7612). AHDB is running its first large-scale strategic review since 2016, and the standalone RB209 app has been retired in favour of web content. [verified via AHDB pages] — [AHDB RB209](https://ahdb.org.uk/nutrient-management-guide-rb209); [RB209 review](https://www.ahdb.org.uk/the-fertiliser-manual-rb209-review). PLANET status was not found. [secondary] — [ifeu](https://www.ifeu.de/en/project/fast-navigator); [Agroportal](https://www.agroportal.pt/fast-navigator-study-identifies-models-necessary-to-provide-accurate-advice-on-the-use-of-fertilisers-to-eu-farmers)

### Inferences
- Gap statement for the paper: regulatory N-balance engines are usually closed spreadsheets or web apps documented in grey literature. Few are open, unit-tested against the official reference, and able to propagate input uncertainty. NFert can be framed as reproducible "rules-as-code" for nutrient compliance. Before claiming novelty, check for other R or Python packages implementing national rules.

### Gaps
- Peer-reviewed descriptions of the DüV calculators, PLANET/MANNER-NPK, and the Danish N-norm system were not retrieved.
- No systematic review of digital nutrient-management or compliance tools was found. A targeted Scopus search is recommended.

## 5. Suggested links for objectives and impact statement

### Takeaway
Position NFert as auditable, open, rules-as-code infrastructure for the "smarter, locally adapted, less burdensome" ND implementation the 2026 evaluation calls for. It is also a candidate engine for FaST-type services under CAP Art. 15(4)(g). Its uncertainty and weather analysis shows that deterministic compliance totals hide a substantial non-compliance risk.

### Inferences (draft wording ideas, not claims)
- **Objective candidates:**
  1. An open, tested reimplementation of the E-R DPI 2026 N balance and MAS (RR 2/2024), validated against Fert_Office/FertDPI. This is an auditable compliance engine.
  2. Quantify how measured in-season rainfall, versus the regulatory rainfall class, changes the balance and compliance status relative to the MAS. In the case study: +26%, 4 of 8 fields over the MAS.
  3. Propagate soil-map uncertainty to field and farm compliance totals, and show that farm-total uncertainty depends on the error correlation length. This matters for how farm-level limits should be checked and how much tolerance is defensible.
  4. Bridge compliance to precision: variable-rate prescriptions that respect the field mean and legal caps.
- **Impact statement hooks:**
  - Supports the "smarter implementation, less admin burden" agenda of the 2026 ND evaluation through reproducible, machine-readable rules (rules-as-code) that advisors, control bodies and FaST-type platforms can reuse.
  - Makes the two-layer Italian system (voluntary DPI balance vs mandatory MAS cap) explicit and checkable. It shows where they conflict under real weather.
  - Turns compliance from a deterministic pass/fail into a risk estimate (probability of exceeding the MAS). This informs inspection targeting and tolerance margins. Frame it as a policy option, not a recommendation.
  - Contributes to Farm to Fork's 50% nutrient-loss target only indirectly. Avoid overclaiming; INMAP was still undelivered in Oct 2026.
- **Caveats to state:** the MAS is a legal cap, not an agronomic optimum. The DPI balance is a regulatory construct, not a crop-need measurement. Exceeding the MAS under the measured-rainfall scenario means the legal cap binds, not that farmers are non-compliant, unless they applied that dose.
